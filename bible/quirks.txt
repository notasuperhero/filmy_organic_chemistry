Character quirks

Every participant in the bible has one fixed personality, and every quirk is a real chemical property. A story may only show a character doing what its quirk allows, so new stories cannot contradict the chemistry. Reaction codes (H1, X3, C7…) point to the Reactions tab.

How the quirks work

1. One core quirk per character, fixed forever. It is the character's most important property, turned into behaviour.
2. Role changes are written down, never improvised. When a character plays different roles (bromine as electrophile, nucleophile, radical or leaving group), each role is listed with the reaction where it happens. A story uses only the listed behaviour for that reaction.
3. Conditions change behaviour, not personality. The same character can act differently in water and in alcohol, or cold and hot. Those switches are listed as "triggers".
4. Transformations are allowed only where chemistry allows them. For example, –CH₃ becomes –COOH with KMnO₄ and its seating rule flips. Each transformation is listed.
5. Every quirk has a "never" line. A story that shows a character doing its "never" is wrong chemistry.
6. Names stay chemical (Sodium, Nitro group, Carbocation). Personality lives in behaviour, not in the name.

Each table below uses the same columns:

- Core quirk: the personality.
- Encodes: the fact behind it.
- In reactions: how the quirk plays out, reaction by reaction.
- Never: what the character can never do.

The benzene ring and the seating rules

The ring: six carbons sharing six electrons as one household (aromatic sextet). Proud of its unity: a newcomer can join only by replacing a hydrogen (substitution); the household is broken for a moment (arenium ion) and immediately restored. Only under force does it accept additions: H₂ with Ni and heat (cyclohexane), Cl₂ in UV light (hexachlorocyclohexane). Never does addition with HBr, Br₂/CCl₄ or Baeyer's reagent.

Seats relative to a group already sitting on the ring: ortho (next to it, positions 2 and 6), meta (one seat away, 3 and 5), para (directly opposite, 4).

When two groups share the ring, the stronger host decides where the next newcomer sits (activators beat deactivators).

Never-break rules

A story that breaks any of these is chemically wrong. Check every new story against this list.

Addition and orientation

1. H⁺ always joins the double-bond carbon that already has more hydrogens (Markovnikov), except HBr with peroxide, or B₂H₆.
2. The peroxide switch works only for HBr.
3. Benzene never takes HBr, Br₂/CCl₄ or Baeyer's reagent by addition; it substitutes.

Substitution and elimination

4. 1° halides go SN2; 3° halides go SN1 or eliminate; 3° never go SN2.
5. SN2 always inverts; SN1 at a chiral carbon always racemises.
6. Aqueous KOH substitutes; alcoholic KOH eliminates.
7. –OH never leaves without first taking H⁺ (or being converted by SOCl₂/PCl₅/HX).
8. Haloarenes never undergo ordinary substitution; they need 623 K/300 atm or –NO₂ at o/p.
9. KCN gives nitriles, AgCN isocyanides; KNO₂ nitrites, AgNO₂ nitro compounds.

Ring seating

10. Germophobes (–NO₂, –CHO, –COR, –COOH, –CN, –SO₃H, –NH₃⁺) send newcomers only to meta.
11. Hosts (–OH, –O⁻, –NH₂, –NHCOCH₃, –OCH₃, alkyl) and halogens send newcomers only to ortho/para.
12. Friedel–Crafts never happens on rings carrying –NO₂, –COOH or –NH₂.

Carbonyls and acids

13. Ketones never pass Tollens' or Fehling's; aromatic aldehydes never pass Fehling's.
14. Aldol needs α-H; Cannizzaro needs no α-H. HCHO is always the one oxidised in crossed Cannizzaro.
15. Iodoform needs CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)–.
16. NaBH₄ never reduces acids or esters; LiAlH₄ never reduces an isolated C=C.
17. Carboxylic acids never give 2,4-DNP or Tollens'. Only acids, not phenols, fizz with NaHCO₃.
18. In esterification the acid loses –OH, the alcohol keeps its oxygen.

Alcohols and ethers

19. 3° alcohols never oxidise with K₂Cr₂O₇; PCC never goes past the aldehyde.
20. Anisole + HI never gives iodobenzene.

Nitrogen

21. Only 1° amines give carbylamine; 3° amines never react with Hinsberg's reagent.
22. Aliphatic diazonium salts never survive; aromatic ones survive only at 273–278 K.
23. Gabriel never makes aromatic amines; Hofmann bromamide always removes one carbon.
24. Aniline never does Friedel–Crafts.

Reagent tempers and conditions

25. Sodium and potassium never meet water calmly; Grignard reagents never survive any acidic H.
26. Cold dilute KMnO₄ adds; hot KMnO₄ breaks; KMnO₄ never touches a side chain without a benzylic H.
27. Lindlar and Pd/BaSO₄ always stop halfway (cis-alkene; aldehyde).
28. Radicals never appear without light, heat, peroxide or electrolysis (Kolbe).
